If you keep only five

  1. Ganascia, L'I.A. expliquée aux humains (2024). A short book, already on the syllabus, to get the history and vocabulary of chapter 1 clear in your mind before the first session (syllabus).
  2. Elements of AI, in French. A free online course with no code: take it yourself before the semester, as it covers the ideas of chapters 1 and 3 (courses).
  3. The Programming Historian en français. Python lessons written for the humanities: the best bridge between your discipline and chapter 2 (digital humanities).
  4. Voyant Tools. Paste a text into the browser and it becomes frequencies, a word cloud and concordances, with an interface in French or Arabic: your first demonstration, with nothing to install (tools).
  5. UNESCO, Orientations pour l'intelligence artificielle générative dans l'éducation et la recherche (2023). In French, English and Arabic: what you need to set a clear rule on the use of ChatGPT and similar tools (ethics).

How to read the entries. The FR, EN and AR labels give the language of the resource or, for a tool, the language of its interface, its documentation or the texts it handles. The last label gives the level: beginner means accessible with no prerequisites or just after chapter 2; intermediate means you need to be comfortable with Python or with the ideas of chapter 3. If a link no longer works, search for the title and the name of the organisation.

Digital humanities and text analysis

The digital humanities do with texts what this course does with surveys: counting, comparing, classifying, linking. Distant reading (lecture distante) studies hundreds of texts at once by computation, instead of reading a few of them closely (see Moretti on the Books shelf). Textometry, a mainly French-speaking tradition, measures how vocabulary is distributed across a corpus. Voyant Tools is presented on the Tools shelf.

Resources in Arabic and for Arabic

Most of the tools in this course were designed for English. With Arabic texts, three difficulties keep coming back: the script is written without short vowels; the morphology attaches the article and pronouns to the word (see chapter 2); and there is a gap between Standard Arabic and the dialects, including Algerian darja, which is written in Arabic or Latin script. The following resources deal with these difficulties. The UNESCO texts on the Ethics shelf also exist in Arabic, for example the Recommandation sur l'éthique de l'intelligence artificielle.

Before students collect data themselves

Questionnaires, online comments, messages: these are personal data under Algerian Law 18-07 (Ethics shelf). Inform the people concerned, keep only what is necessary, and anonymise before analysis. Also, a text that is online is not necessarily free of copyright: prefer the public domain and open licences, and always make students cite the source.
