Teaching objectives

  • Mastery of AI algorithms for the textual processing of surveys
  • Ability to use the Python software,
  • Understand the approaches of AI in problem solving,
  • Ability to process a corpus of texts.

Target knowledge

  • Identify the opportunities of artificial intelligence in the humanities and social sciences.
  • Understand what the different applications of AI for the humanities and social sciences (SHS) can be.
  • Understand the ethical implications of AI and good practices in its use.
  • Ability to carry out the Clustering of social behaviors in surveys and predictive modeling.
  • Understand sentiments on networks and how to analyze them.
  • Ability to extract texts from political speeches.
  • Ability to analyze sociological networks.

Teaching method

  • Lectures.
  • Tutorials (TD).
  • Lab sessions (TP).
  • Mini-project (complete at least 3 of the 5 proposed subjects)
  • Maximum teacher – student interactivity.
  • Independent work (especially for programming).
  • Assessment (identical to the regulations in force).
  • Round tables (Genesis and emergence of AI, Impact of AI on thinking and methodologies in the humanities and social sciences (SHS), AI as a vector of new scientific and societal issues, Outlook for AI, etc.).

Subject content

Chapter 1: Introduction

  • Introduction to Artificial intelligence (AI) (1 week, if insufficient use 1 lab session (TP) to complete)
    • Historical overview of artificial intelligence,
    • Narrow (or weak) AI
    • General (or strong) AI
  • Supervised learning and unsupervised learning
    • The approaches of AI in problem solving,
    • Outlook.

Chapter 2: Review and Further topics on the language (2 weeks)

  • The fundamentals
  • The libraries Pandas, Numpy, etc.
  • Introduction to object-oriented programming,
  • Read and process text files (search, sort)
  • manage simple reports (PDF, Excel)

Chapter 3: Types of AI and Approaches (6 weeks)

  • Symbolic AI (expert system)
  • Machine Learning (Machine Learning)
    • Machine Learning (ML), with examples such as: Classification of emails as "spam" or "non-spam", recommendations, image identification (e.g.: faces), etc.
  • Deep Learning (Deep Learning)
    • Deep Learning
    • Text generation with Transformers (or other).
  • AI by Reinforcement (Renforcement Learning)
    • Reinforcement learning (RL)

Mini-project

Mini-project (3 weeks – supervised independent work – to be handed out at the end of chapter 2):

Work on 1 subject of your choice from:

Subject 1. Sentiment Analysis on Social Networks

Objective: Analyze text corpora coming from social networks (Twitter, Facebook, etc.) to determine users' sentiments about a particular topic (e.g.: a political campaign, a health crisis, or a social event).

Tools: Python, TextBlob, VADER, Tweepy (for Twitter), pandas, matplotlib, GeoPandas.

Subject 2. Clustering of Social Behaviors in Surveys

Objective: Use clustering techniques to group individuals with similar social behaviors based on survey data. This can be useful for segmenting a population into distinct interest groups.

Tools: Python, pandas, sklearn, matplotlib, seaborn, scikit-learn.

Subject 3. Text Analysis for Information Extraction in Political Speeches

Objective: Use AI to extract main themes and named entities (names of people, places, dates) from political speeches. This can be used to understand the concerns of politicians and analyze their rhetoric.

Tools: Python, spaCy, Transformers (BERT), gensim (LDA), matplotlib, WordCloud.

Subject 4. Predictive Modeling of Social Behaviors from Consumption Data

Objective: Build a predictive model to estimate consumers' social behaviors (for example, the probability of purchasing products or of participating in social events) based on various variables.

Tools: Python, sklearn, pandas, matplotlib, seaborn.

Subject 5. Analysis of Social Networks (Sociology of Networks)

Objective: Use social network analysis techniques (Social Network Analysis - SNA) to study the relationships between individuals in a social network, using structured data.

Tools: Python, NetworkX, Gephi, matplotlib, pandas.

Tutorials (TD) / Lab sessions (TP)

Chapter 1:

  1. Go over the notions seen in the course (chapter 1) and deepen the notion of supervised and unsupervised learning
  2. What is the difference between supervised learning and unsupervised learning?
  3. Give examples of learning
  4. Case to do as independent work:

    Study the literature on AI in order to determine whether current computers can carry out the following operations:

    • Play ping-pong properly
    • Drive a car in the center of a city
    • Discover and prove mathematical theorems
    • Write a funny story
    • Give a relevant opinion in a given legal field
    • Perform a complex surgical operation

Chapter 2:

  • Exercises in Python: programs, lists and tuples, data manipulation with the libraries seen in the course.

Chapter 3:

  1. Exercise on the expert system:
    • Consider the following rules in forward chaining:
    • R1 : A → E
    • R2 : B → D
    • R3 : H → A ∩ F
    • R4 : E ∩ G → C
    • R5 : E ∩ K → B
    • R6 : D ∩ E ∩ K → C
    • R7 : G ∩ K ∩ F → A

    Order of the rules: R3 R7 R1 R4 R5 R2 R6
    Non-deducible facts: H, G, K
    Initial fact base: H, K
    Goal: C

    Questions:
    Draw the graph corresponding to the rules
    Work through the system in forward chaining.

  2. Introduce some algorithms used in ML:
    1. Linear regression,
    2. Gradient descent
    3. Classification into categories based on attributes
    4. Grouping of similar objects into clusters
    5. Etc.
  3. Give examples of classification, recommendations, image identification.
  4. Exercises on each type of learning:
    1. ML
    2. Deep learning (CNN, RNN)
    3. reinforcement

Lab sessions (TP)

  • The lab sessions (TP) will be reserved for programming the exercises seen in the tutorials (TD) using Python and for carrying out a mini-project.
