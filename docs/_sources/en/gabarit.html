Course objectives

  • Master AI algorithms for processing the texts of surveys (ch. 3).
  • Be able to use Python (ch. 2).
  • Understand how AI approaches problem solving (ch. 1).
  • Be able to process a corpus of texts (ch. 2).

Teaching method

  • Lectures, tutorials (TD) and lab sessions (TP) (TD/TP).
  • Mini-project: complete at least 3 of the 5 subjects offered (see the note).
  • As much teacher–student interaction as possible.
  • Personal work, especially for programming.
  • Assessment according to the regulations in force.
  • Round tables (4 themes).

Target knowledge

  • Identify the opportunities of artificial intelligence in the humanities and social sciences (ch. 1).
  • Understand the different possible applications of AI for the humanities and social sciences (ch. 3).
  • Understand the ethical implications of AI and good practice in using it (ch. 1).
  • Cluster social behaviours in surveys and build predictive models (subjects 2 and 4).
  • Understand the sentiments expressed on social networks and know how to analyse them (subject 1).
  • Extract information from political speeches (subject 3).
  • Analyse sociological networks (subject 5).

Calendar

The semester at a glance

Nine weeks of lessons (1 + 2 + 6), a three-week mini-project handed out at the end of chapter 2, and tutorials and lab sessions throughout the semester.

W1W2W3 W4W5W6 W7W8W9 Chapter 1 1 wk Chapter 2 Python · 2 wks Chapter 3 Types of AI and approaches · 6 weeks Mini-project 3 wks (indicative) TD / TP Exercises on the current chapter, then Python programming and the mini-project

Chapter lengths are those of the syllabus. If one week is not enough for chapter 1, the syllabus allows a lab session to complete it. The syllabus only says that the mini-project is handed out at the end of chapter 2: the teacher sets its exact place in the calendar.

Bibliography

References

The syllabus references, formatted. Typos in the original document are corrected and flagged.

  1. Inglehart, R. (1997). Modernization and Postmodernization: Cultural, Economic, and Political Change in 43 Societies. Princeton University Press.

    The theory of materialist and post-materialist values; a frame for reading the survey data of subjects 2 and 4.

  2. "Western values are diverging from the rest of the world's". The Economist, 2023.

    The syllabus writes "The Economiste". A press article based on World Values Survey data.

  3. Western Michigan University. World Values Survey. wmich.edu/economics/world-values-survey

    An introduction to the World Values Survey. The data itself is downloaded from worldvaluessurvey.org.

  4. Ganascia, J.-G. (2024). L'IA expliquée aux humains. Paris: Éditions du Seuil.

    An accessible introduction (in French), useful for chapter 1 and the round tables.

  5. Langlois, L., Dilhac, M.-A., Dratwa, J. et al. (2023). L'éthique au cœur de l'IA. Québec: Obvia.

    The syllabus writes "Anglais, Lise, Dilhac, Antione"; the names are corrected from the Obvia publication.

  6. Robert, J. (2024). "Natural Language Processing (NLP) : définition et principes". DataScientest. datascientest.com/introduction-au-nlp-natural-language-processing

  7. "What is natural language processing (NLP)?". Amazon Web Services. aws.amazon.com/what-is/nlp (French version: aws.amazon.com/fr/what-is/nlp)

  8. Belhadef, H., Djemal, I. Méthode TALN. Course, University of M'sila, Algeria.

    TALN (traitement automatique des langues naturelles) is the French term for NLP.

  9. Prieur, B. Traitement automatique du langage naturel avec Python. Le NLP avec spaCy et NLTK.

    A practical reference (in French) for chapter 2 and subject 3.

  10. Baddari, K., Herzallah, A. (2015). La recherche de l'information. Algiers: Office des publications universitaires (OPU).
